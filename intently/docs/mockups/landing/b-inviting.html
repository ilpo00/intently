Why this direction

Philosophy: the empty right canvas isn't a hole to hide — it's the most valuable real estate on the screen, and right now it's wasted on a compass icon. So fill it with the one thing that makes Intently worth choosing over the product grid: a real, dimmed, slowly-breathing example of the payoff — the understood situation, the "best match," and the why-this line that no filter page can produce. You sell the difference by showing it, before the shopper has spent a single word.

Best for

The filter-fatigued shopper who just bounced off the PLP, and the browser with no fixed need. Both have to be shown a reason to type — this is the highest-pull direction.

Layout

Left-weighted, asymmetric. Copy + CTA on the left (reading order), the living preview on the right — the same two-pane geometry the post-search state already uses, so it feels continuous.

Keeps the chips

Twice. Seed chips under the input (to start), and the green "understood" context chips inside the preview (to demonstrate the comprehension moment that makes this feel like being heard).

On "calm reveal"

This is where your instinct pays off most: the preview cross-fades between situations on a slow, calm cycle. It's ambient, never demanding — the motion is the invitation.

What each marker does

  1. Live input, immediately present. Ringed in accent, pre-focused, reading-order first. The preview pulls the eye; the input catches it.
  2. The dead canvas is now a value demo. Instead of "your results will appear here," the shopper sees what results actually look like here — short, reasoned, decisive.
  3. "Example" tag + dimming. The single most important guardrail: the preview is clearly a sample, not live results, so no one taps "Add" expecting it to be theirs. Honesty keeps the trick from backfiring.
  4. The "understood" chips. A preview of Intently's signature beat — "I read your situation and here's what I caught." This is the emotional hook that filters can't touch.
  5. Seeds fade in calmly as the secondary layer, lowering activation energy without crowding the headline.
  6. The why-line is the hero of the card — accent rule, full sentence, rooted in the situation. It's the proof that this is reasoning, not retrieval.
My recommendation: ship this one (or this + A's restraint). It's the only direction that directly answers your two named failure modes. "Just a different way to the same results?" — no, here's the explained shortlist, visible up front. "A dead end people abandon for the PLP?" — the payoff is the first thing they see, so the pull runs toward typing.
The honest risk. A live-looking preview can mislead — hence the dimming and the "Example" tag, which you must not drop to look cleaner. Second: a cycling panel can read as a carousel ad if the motion is even slightly too fast or too bright; keep it slow (~4s), low-contrast, one card at a time. Third — and this applies to all three — the landing writes a cheque the first real message has to cash. If the actual first shortlist looks like a 30-tile PLP, this preview makes the let-down worse, not better. That's the next phase, and it's the one that decides whether any of this works.
