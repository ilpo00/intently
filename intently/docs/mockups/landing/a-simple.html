Why this direction

Philosophy: the fastest way to fix "too empty" is to stop rendering an empty thing. Before the first search there is no two-pane split and no faint compass in a void — just one calm, centred composition where the input is the page. The results canvas is born the moment it has something to hold. This is the lowest-risk direction: it reads as a confident, premium prompt rather than an unfinished dashboard.

Best for

The decisive shopper and the confident first-timer — people who already have a sentence in their head and just need an obvious, dignified place to put it. Also the safest default if you ship one.

Layout

Centred. Single focal column, generous air. The post-submit state slides into the familiar conversation-rail + results-canvas split — so this is purely a landing treatment, not an engine change.

Keeps the chips

The three situation seeds move from a vertical stack in a cramped 380px rail to a calm horizontal row directly under the input, where they read as "ways to start," not a menu.

On "calm reveal"

Applied — but deliberately to the secondary layer only. The input is present and focused at t=0; the seeds and the promise line fade in over the next second. See the candour note.

What each marker does

  1. The input is the hero, not a footer. Today the only text field is the small refine bar pinned to the bottom of the left rail — easy to miss on arrival. Here it's enlarged, centred, pre-focused, and ringed in the pine accent so the eye lands on it first. The blinking caret says "type here" without a word.
  2. Seeds as a horizontal row, fading in calmly. They lower activation energy (the hardest moment is the blank box) and double as a worked example of the kind of sentence that works — situation, not SKU.
  3. (reserved) — the empty results canvas that exists today is simply not rendered in this state.
  4. A rotating, situational placeholder. It cycles real situations ("a linen shirt for a warm coastal wedding…", "sturdy track pants that don't look like gym clothes…") so even an empty field is teaching what to say.
  5. A one-line promise that pre-empts the killer objection. "A curated few — each with the reason it fits" tells a filter-fatigued shopper, before they invest a word, that this is not another endless results page.
  6. No void. The ~800px empty canvas that prompted this whole exercise doesn't appear until there's a shortlist to put in it.
Where I'd push back / the honest trade-off. This is the least "wow" of the three — it leans on copy and calm to sell the difference, so a hardened skeptic might still read it as "a nicer search box." It also does the least to show the payoff up front (Concept B does that). I built the calm reveal CTA-first on purpose: a shopper who's two seconds from bouncing to the old product grid must see the input and the promise immediately — only the suggestions earn the gentle delay. Fading in the primary action would be the one place "calm" turns into "broken."
