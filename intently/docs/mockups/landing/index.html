The problem, precisely

Two-thirds of the screen is a void

Before the first search, the desktop layout is a 380px | 1fr split: a cramped invitation in the left rail, and the entire right two-thirds rendered as an empty canvas with a faint compass and one line of text. The only input is the small bar pinned to the bottom of the rail. It reads as an unfinished dashboard, not an invitation.

What's cold about it
  • The void dominates. ~800px of empty canvas is the largest element on screen — the eye reads "broken / not loaded" before "calm."
  • The input hides. The one thing we want tapped is a small bar at the bottom of a side rail — not the focal point.
  • No reason to prefer it. Nothing on screen shows why this beats the filters the shopper already knows. It looks like search with extra steps.
  • High activation energy. A blank field + a faint hint asks the shopper to do all the work of imagining what's possible.

Five situations that bring someone to "shop by situation"

The landing has to work for very different states of mind. The two marked ● are the ones the redesign must win — they're where the old product-listing page already failed the shopper, which is exactly the moment Intently earns or loses them.

The filter refugee ●

Trigger: just bounced off the PLP

Applied filters, got 240 results or 0, ping-ponged between category pages, never felt "yes, that." Arrives frustrated. "I've tried; show me you can do better than the filters I just gave up on."

Needs the payoff shown up front
→ best served by B

The blank-box freezer ●

Trigger: lands, doesn't know what to type

Open to inspiration or shopping for someone else ("something for my dad who hikes"), but an empty field is the same open-ended task that overwhelms. "Give me a door to walk through."

Needs concrete starting points
→ best served by C

The constraint-stacker

Trigger: a need filters can't express

Knows the shape of it — "sturdy track pants that don't look like gym clothes," "a linen shirt for a warm coastal wedding." Function + aesthetic + context in one breath. "Let me say it as a sentence."

Needs a clean place to type
→ best served by A

The confident first-timer

Trigger: arrives with intent

Has an occasion in mind, doesn't know this store's taxonomy. Filters require knowing the catalogue's words; a situation doesn't. "I'll tell you the occasion — you map it to products."

Needs low friction + a clear promise
→ A or B

The curious browser

Trigger: no fixed need, just looking

Might type, might not. Will leave if there's nothing to react to. "Show me something interesting and I'll engage."

Needs something alive on arrival
→ B or C

Shared principles

What all three directions hold to

However the layout varies, every direction obeys the same five rules — drawn from the diagnosis and your brief.

1 · Don't render a void

The empty results canvas never shows before there's a shortlist to fill it. Each direction puts something living, useful, or calm where the compass used to sit.

2 · The input is the hero

One unmistakable, pre-focused field ringed in the pine accent — never a small bar hiding in a rail.

3 · Show the difference, don't claim it

Every direction signals "explained shortlist, not 300 results" — through a promise line (A), a live preview (B), or a stated 3-step consultation (C).

4 · Lower activation energy

The chips/seeds stay — repositioned so the hardest moment (the blank box) always has an easy first move close at hand.

5 · Calm reveal — but CTA-first

Suggestions arrive gently, staggered. The primary input and promise are present at t=0. Calm belongs to the secondary layer, never the core action.

0 · Stay unmistakably Intently

Warm paper, one pine accent, Cormorant + Inter, the explained card. The variation is layout and approach — not a rebrand.

My read

Where I'd put the chips

Lead with B, borrow A's restraint.

B is the only direction that meets your two hard requirements head-on. "Is this just a different way to the same results?" — no: the explained shortlist is the first thing on screen. "Will it be a dead end people abandon for the PLP?" — the pull runs toward typing, because the reward is visible before the cost.

Keep B's restraint dialled to A's level: one preview card, slow cross-fade, lots of air. If you can only ship one, ship B. If you want a fallback that's nearly impossible to get wrong, A. Reach for C only if behaviour data shows people landing and leaving without typing — that's the specific disease C cures, and it over-assists everyone else.

Two things you didn't ask me to say

On "calmly appearing suggestions" — I agree, with one edit. Calm reveals feel premium and cut overwhelm, so they're in all three. But a filter-refugee is ~2 seconds from leaving. If the input or the promise fades in on a delay, you've hidden the lifeline at the exact moment it's needed. So: primary action present instantly, suggestions fade in. That's the one place where "calm" and "broken" look identical, and the difference is which layer you delay.

A landing can't save a weak first answer. All three write the same cheque — "a curated few, each with a reason." If the first real shortlist comes back looking like a 30-tile product grid, the better the landing, the harder the let-down. The landing is necessary and not sufficient; the first message is where this is actually won or lost.
